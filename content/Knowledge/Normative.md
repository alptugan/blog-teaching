In academic contexts, **normativity** means the study or use of standards, rules, or values about what should be, ought to be, or count as justified. It appears in fields like philosophy, law, education, linguistics, and the social sciences, where scholars ask not only what is true or factual, but also what is appropriate, valid, or warranted. [rep.routledge](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/thematic/normativity/v-1)

## Core meaning
Normativity is about **“ought” questions** rather than just “is” questions. For example, an argument about epistemic normativity asks what makes a belief rational or justified, while an ethical discussion asks what actions are right or wrong. [academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/book/9344/chapter/156128072)

## In academia
Different disciplines use the term a bit differently:

- In philosophy, it often refers to reasons, obligations, justification, and standards of rationality.[academic.oup](https://academic.oup.com/book/9344/chapter/156128072)
    
- In education, it can refer to rules or expectations that guide teaching and learning practices.[sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877042813030619/pdf?md5=667e9b3f386e6e22cc0a8741b63c3488&pid=1-s2.0-S1877042813030619-main.pdf)
    
- In law and social theory, it can describe norms that structure behavior and institutions.[journals.openedition](https://journals.openedition.org/revus/4773)

## Simple example
A descriptive claim says, “Students usually cite sources in essays.” A normative claim says, “Students should cite sources in essays.” The second statement is normative because it sets a standard. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normativity)

## Why it matters
Academic writing often distinguishes between describing reality and evaluating it. Normativity matters because many scholarly debates are not only about what happens, but also about what should count as correct, fair, rational, or acceptable. [sk.sagepub](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/encyclopedia-of-philosophy-and-the-social-sciences/chpt/normativity)


## Turkish Notes
Normatif ifadeler, olgusal (descriptive) ifadelerden farklıdır. Olgusal ifadeler, bir şeyin nasıl olduğunu tanımlarken, normatif ifadeler bir şeyin nasıl olması gerektiğini belirtir. Örneğin, "Sigara içmek sağlığa zararlıdır" olgusal bir ifadedir, ancak "Sigara içmek yasaklanmalıdır" normatif bir ifadedir.

Özetle, normatiflik, **değer yargıları, standartlar ve beklentiler** aracılığıyla bir şeyin nasıl olması gerektiğine dair bir çerçeve sunar ve çeşitli disiplinlerde rehberlik edici bir rol oynar.
