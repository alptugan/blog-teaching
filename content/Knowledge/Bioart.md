Bioart is a form of contemporary art that uses living systems, biology, or biotechnology as its medium, such as bacteria, cells, DNA, or other biological processes. It often blends art and science to explore questions about life, ethics, identity, and the relationship between humans and nature. [adobe](https://www.adobe.com/uk/express/learn/blog/what-is-bio-art)

## What it involves
Bioart can include:
- Working with living organisms in labs, like bacteria or cells. [amino](https://amino.bio/blogs/what-is/what-is-bioart)
- Using biotech themes or materials, such as genetics or tissue engineering. [adobe](https://www.adobe.com/uk/express/learn/blog/what-is-bio-art)
- Creating works that change over time because the material is alive. [amino](https://amino.bio/blogs/what-is/what-is-bioart)

## Why artists use it
Artists use bioart to make people think about scientific advances and their ethical implications, not just to create something visually unusual. It can also challenge the boundary between what counts as natural, artificial, living, and nonliving. [adobe](https://www.adobe.com/uk/express/learn/blog/what-is-bio-art)

## Simple example
A bioart piece might be a glowing organism grown in a lab, or an artwork made from living bacteria arranged on a dish. [adobe](https://www.adobe.com/uk/express/learn/blog/what-is-bio-art)
