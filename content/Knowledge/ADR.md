ADR stands for Automated Dialogue Replacement. It is a post-production process used in filmmaking where actors re-record their dialogue in a controlled studio environment. This technique is primarily employed to enhance audio quality or to make changes to the script after filming has concluded.

## Purpose of ADR
### Key Reasons for Using ADR
- Improving Audio Quality: Background noise or technical issues during filming can compromise the clarity of dialogue. ADR allows filmmakers to capture cleaner audio.
- Script Changes: If there are modifications to the script after filming, ADR enables actors to record new lines that align with the updated dialogue.
    
- Performance Enhancement: Actors can refine their performances, ensuring that the emotional tone and delivery match the director's vision.

### Common Scenarios for ADR
- Unintelligible Dialogue: When lines are unclear due to noise or poor recording conditions.
- Wide Shots: In scenes where the camera is far from the actors, capturing clear dialogue can be challenging, making ADR necessary.
    
- Accents and Delivery: If an actor's accent slips or their delivery needs adjustment, ADR can correct these issues.

## Process of Recording ADR
1. Identifying Cues: Sound editors review the film to determine which lines need re-recording.
2. Recording Session: Actors perform their lines in a soundproof studio, often while watching the original footage to match timing and emotion.
    
3. Editing and Mixing: The newly recorded dialogue is blended with the original audio track to create a seamless final product.


This process is crucial for achieving high-quality sound in films, ensuring that dialogue is clear and engaging for the audience.
