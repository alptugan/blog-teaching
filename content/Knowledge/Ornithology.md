Ornithology is the branch of zoology focused on the scientific study of birds. This field examines various aspects of birds, including:

- Behavior: How birds interact with each other and their environment.
- Ecology: The relationships between birds and their habitats.
- Evolution: The development and adaptation of bird species over time.

## Etymology
The term "ornithology" derives from the Greek words:

- Ornis: Meaning "bird"
- Logos: Meaning "study" or "discourse"

This reflects the field's focus on understanding birds scientifically.

## Historical Context
Ornithology has a rich history, with early observations of birds dating back to the Stone Age. The formal study began to take shape in the late 1600s, moving from folklore and myths to a more scientific approach. Key figures in its development include:

- John Ray: Combined natural history and systematics in the late 1600s.
- Francis Willughby: Focused on biology, anatomy, and behavior in his works.
- Carl Linnaeus: Established a classification system for all organisms, including birds.

## Importance of Ornithology
The study of birds has significantly contributed to our understanding of ecological principles, species interactions, and conservation efforts. Ornithologists play a crucial role in protecting bird species and their habitats, making this field vital for biodiversity and environmental health.
