 Drawing on the concept of acoustemology, **Feld (2012, 2015)** conceptualizes sonic engagement as a relational, knowledge-producing process rather than a passive mechanism for data capture. **Robinson (2020)** expands this framework through an Indigenous studies lens, critiquing conventional academic and artistic approaches to sound as instances of "*hungry listening*"—a settler-colonial epistemology that reduces acoustic phenomena to extractable, categorizable, and possessable resources. In response to this extractive paradigm, Robinson advocates for a *resonant listening* practice, wherein researchers acknowledge both the inherent agency of sound and their own positionality as guests within the acoustic environment.
