## Key Contributions
### 1. The Cartesian Coordinate System (Mathematics)
Descartes invented **analytic geometry**, which connects algebra and geometry. By introducing orthogonal axes—what we now call the Cartesian plane ($x$ and $y$ coordinates)—he made it possible to represent geometric shapes with algebraic equations and vice versa. This laid the direct groundwork for Isaac Newton and Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz to invent calculus.

### 2. _"Cogito, Ergo Sum"_ (Philosophy)
In his landmark works _Discourse on the Method_ (1637) and _Meditations on First Philosophy_ (1641), Descartes set out to find absolute certainty by doubting everything that could possibly be doubted (known as **Cartesian doubt**).

He realized that even if an all-powerful deceiver was fooling all his senses, he must still exist as a conscious entity to be deceived in the first place. He summarized this fundamental truth as:

> _"Cogito, ergo sum"_ — **"I think, therefore I am."**

### 3. Mind-Body Dualism
Descartes argued that reality consists of two distinct types of substance:

- **Res cogitans** (thinking substance): The non-physical mind or soul.
    
- **Res extensa** (extended substance): The physical body and material world.

This concept, known as [[Cartesian Dualism]], shaped centuries of debate in metaphysics, cognitive science, and the philosophy of mind regarding how a non-physical mind interacts with a physical body.
