### Slide 3: Movement & Transition Sounds
# 1. Whooshes, Swooshes, Whips & Swishes
Movement-based sound effects are foundational, particularly in motion graphics and explainer videos.

* **Whooshes & Swooshes:**
  * The most frequently used SFX in explainer videos.
  * Can vary in duration (long or short) to match the speed of visual transitions.
* **Whips & Swishes:**
  * Functionally similar to whooshes.
  * Typically shorter and sharper, ideal for quick cuts or fast object movements.

---

### Slide 4: Accent Sounds & Keywords
# 2. Beeps, Bells & Pop Sounds
Small, detailed sounds used to highlight specific visual actions or on-screen elements.

* **Function:** Serve as "accent sounds" to draw the viewer's attention to micro-interactions.
* **Effective Search Keywords:**
  * `UI sound effects` (User Interface)
  * `Technology sound effects`
  * `Game audio` / `Game SFX`

---

### Slide 5: Building Tension & Release
# 3. Swells, Risers & Impacts
Dynamic sound design relies on manipulating audience expectations through tension and release.

* **Swells & Risers:**
  * Used to escalate tension and build anticipation leading up to a key moment or transition.
* **Impacts:**
  * Placed immediately after a riser.
  * Delivers the sonic "punch" or payoff that the audience anticipates.

---

### Slide 6: Environmental Context
# 4. Ambience
Background audio that establishes the setting and grounds the visual scene in reality.

* **Examples:** A quiet forest, a vivid street, a busy bar, or an airport terminal.
* **Design Value:** Every environment has a unique sonic signature; layering ambience adds depth, realism, and immersion to a scene.

---

### Slide 7: Professional Audio Libraries
# Sourcing SFX: Industry Platforms
When searching for professional assets, look for platforms specializing in **"Stock Music"** or **"Sound Effects."**

* **Epidemic Sound** (`epidemicsound.com`)
* **Splice** (`splice.com`)
* **Artlist** (`artlist.io`)

*Note: These subscription-based platforms provide properly licensed, royalty-free assets suitable for commercial projects.*

---

### Slide 8: Practice & Free Resources
# Sourcing SFX: Student & Practice Options
For classroom exercises, prototypes, or non-commercial practice, alternative resources are available:

* **YouTube Audio Library:** 
  * Accessible via YouTube Studio; offers categorized, copyright-safe sound effects.
* **Standard YouTube Search:** 
  * Useful for finding specific sounds (e.g., searching `whoosh sfx` or `ui sfx`) strictly for **practice projects**.
  * *Caution:* Standard YouTube uploads are often copyrighted and should not be used in commercial or public portfolios without clearance.

---

### Slide 9: Summary & Discussion
# Tips
1. Match the duration and energy of **transitions** (whooshes vs. whips) to the visual pacing.
2. Use **UI and game audio keywords** to find clean accent sounds.
3. Pair **risers with impacts** to structure dramatic moments.
4. Never neglect **ambience**, which glues the soundscape together.

### Slide 11: Lab Exercise 1
# Lab 1: The SFX Scavenger Hunt
**Goal:** Build a categorized "Starter SFX Kit" using effective search keywords and proper file management.

* **Instructions:**
  1. Access the **YouTube Audio Library** (or use trial/student accounts on *Splice*, *Artlist*, or *Epidemic Sound*).
  2. Source and download **2 distinct audio files** for each of the following categories (10 files total):
     * **Long Transitions:** Search `whoosh`, `swoosh`
     * **Short/Fast Transitions:** Search `whip`, `swish`
     * **Accents:** Search `UI`, `technology sfx`, `game audio`, `pop`, `bell`
     * **Tension & Release:** 1 `riser`/`swell` + 1 `impact`
     * **Environment:** 2 contrasting `ambience` tracks (e.g., forest vs. busy street)
* **Deliverable:** A zipped folder organized by sub-folders with standardized file naming (e.g., `Category_Descriptor_Source.wav`) and a text log noting the license type (Commercial vs. Practice-only).

### Slide 12: Lab Exercise 2
# Lab 2: Re-Sounding a Muted Explainer
**Goal:** Synchronize movement, accent, and tension SFX to a 15–30 second muted motion graphics clip.

* **Setup:** Import the provided muted animation clip into your DAW.
* **Required Track Layers:**
  1. **Ambience Bed (1–2 tracks):** Establish the spatial environment from start to finish.
  2. **Macro Movement (1–2 tracks):** Sync *whooshes* to slow camera pans and *whips/swishes* to fast cuts.
  3. **Micro Accents (2–3 tracks):** Place *beeps, bells, or pops* on small on-screen text or icon animations.
  4. **Dramatic Peak (2 tracks):** Build up to the final logo reveal or key visual moment using a *swell/riser* that resolves into a hard *impact*.
* **Focus:** Pay close attention to **timing** (matching the peak of the whoosh to the fastest frame of movement) and **gain staging** (ensuring ambience doesn't mask the accents).

### Slide 13: Lab Exercise 3
# Lab 3: Context Shifting (Comparative Study)
**Goal:** Demonstrate how ambience and accent choices alter the narrative perception of the exact same visual.

* **Instructions:**
  * Take a 10-second loop of a simple geometric animation.
  * **Version A (Organic/Calm):** Score the clip using natural ambience (e.g., park, wind, birds) and soft, organic accents (e.g., wood pops, soft bells).
  * **Version B (Sci-Fi/High-Tech):** Score the *exact same clip* using industrial/tech ambience (e.g., server room, busy airport) and synthetic accents (e.g., `UI` beeps, glitch sounds, heavy sub-impacts).
* **Class Discussion:** Present both versions back-to-back and analyze how the audio changes the perceived weight, speed, and mood of the visuals.

### Slide 14: Evaluation & Peer Critique
# Lab Assessment Criteria
When reviewing your work (and your peers' projects), evaluate the following:

| Criteria | Key Questions | Weight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sync & Pacing** | Do the durations of the *whooshes* and *whips* match the speed of the visual transitions? | 30% |
| **Sonic Variety** | Are *UI/accent* sounds varied enough to avoid listener fatigue (the "machine gun" effect)? | 25% |
| **Dynamics & Flow** | Does the *riser-to-impact* transition feel earned and properly timed? | 25% |
| **Mix & Ambience** | Does the *ambience* glue the track together without overpowering the foreground SFX? | 20% |
