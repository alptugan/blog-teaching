# Datavis
1. **Typography:** Data requires rigorous typographic hierarchy (Posters/Infographics).
2. **Video:** Modern data storytelling relies heavily on Motion Graphics and "explainer" videos.
3. **Sound:** Data Sonification (representing data with sound) is a cutting-edge field that fulfills the "Sound" requirement.
4. **Internet:** The final output can be a "Scrollytelling" web experience, covering the web skills.
    

You are not just learning "charts"; you are learning **Information Architecture** across print, time-based media, and interactive web.

# COD 301: Design Studio III – Multimedia Data Narratives
**Theme:** _From Abstract Numbers to Human Stories_
## 1. Course Rationale
This studio course fulfills the COD 301 requirement to "communicate a message by using sound, video, and typography" by treating **Data** as the primary narrative material. Instead of viewing data visualization as merely making charts, students will explore data as a medium for storytelling across three distinct outputs: a **Static Poster** (Print), a **Kinetic Video** (Motion), and an **Interactive Archive** (Web/Sound).

## 2. Required Reference Library
The following texts are essential for this course. Weekly readings are assigned from these volumes to ground studio practice in theoretical rigor.

- **The Truthful Art: Data, Charts, and Maps for Communication** by Alberto Cairo (New Riders, 2016).
    
    - _Why:_ The standard text for understanding data journalism and the architecture of information.
        
- **Dear Data** by Giorgia Lupi and Stefanie Posavec (Princeton Architectural Press, 2016).
    
    - _Why:_ A manifesto for "Data Humanism"—essential for teaching students to see data in their daily lives before using software.
        
- **The Soundscape: Our Sonic Environment and the Tuning of the World** by R. Murray Schafer (Destiny Books, 1993/Original 1977).
    
    - _Why:_ The foundational text for Acoustic Ecology, necessary for the sound design module.
        
- **Sustainable Web Design** by Tom Greenwood (A Book Apart, 2021).
    
    - _Why:_ Critical for the "Portfolio/Web" module, shifting focus from decoration to performance and ecology.
        
- **Designing Interface Animation** by Val Head (Rosenfeld Media, 2016).
    
    - _Why:_ Bridges the gap between static graphic design and motion graphics.
        
- _Data Sketches: A Journey of Imagination, Exploration, and Beautiful Data Visualizations_ **Authors:** Nadieh Bremer & Shirley Wu (2021)

- **"Information is Beautiful"** by David McCandless

## 3. Assessment Rubric (Total 100%)
- **Visual System (25%):** Quality of typography, color usage, and graphical consistency across media.
    
- **Narrative Clarity (25%):** Can the audience understand the data? Is the insight clear?
    
- **Technical Execution (25%):** Quality of the Motion Graphics (smoothness) and Sound (clarity/sync).
    
- **Process & Web Archive (25%):** Quality of the documentation and the final web page (Markdown/HTML structure).


## 4. Weekly Syllabus & Studio Exercises

### Phase 1: The Static Image (Typography & Visual Semiotics)

_Goal: Master the grammar of graphics and static composition._

**Week 1: Data Humanism & The Quantified Self**
- **Topic:** "Small Data" vs. Big Data. How to collect data without computers.
- **Reading:** *Dear Data* (Read the Introduction and browse the first 10 weeks of postcards, Giorgia Lupi).
- **Studio Exercise:** _The Pocket Audit._ Students strictly use pen and paper to track one personal habit for 7 days (e.g., "Times I complained," "Colors I saw," "Music I listened to").
- **Assignment:** Compile the raw data and bring physical sketches to class.
    

**Week 2: The Grammar of Graphics**

- **Topic:** Visual variables (Position, Size, Color, Texture, Shape) and encoding data.
    
- **Reading:** *The Truthful Art*, Chapter 2: "The Five Qualities of Great Visualizations."
    
- **Reading:** _Data Sketches_, **"Olympic Feathers"** (Pages 42–65) and **"Figures in the Sky"** (Pages 180–205).
    
    - _Focus:_ Notice how the authors refuse to use standard bar charts. Look at how they invent new visual metaphors (feathers, constellations) to represent hard numbers.
        
- **Studio Exercise:** _99 Variations._
    
    - Take one simple data point from your Week 1 collection (e.g., "5 apples").
        
    - Sketch it in 20 different visual styles (Abstract, Geometric, Organic, Glitch, Minimalist) using Adobe Illustrator/Figma.
        
    - _Constraint:_ You cannot use a bar, line, or pie chart. You must invent a shape.
        
- **Assignment:** Digitize Week 1 data into a clean CSV file and produce 3 low-fidelity poster layouts based on one of your "99 Variations" sketches.
    

**Week 3: Typography as Interface**

- **Topic:** Hierarchy in information design. The "Read-Glance-Deep Dive" structure.
    
- **Reading:** *The Truthful Art*, Chapter 5: "Basic Principles of Visualization" (Focus on scales and coordinates, pg. 127).
    
- **Studio Exercise:** _The Annotation Layer._ Students exchange raw charts and practice adding "active titles" and annotations that explain _why_ the data matters, not just _what_ it is.
    
- **Project Launch:** The A1 Data Poster.
    

**Week 4: The Static Critique (Pin-Up)**

- **Topic:** Print production and scale.
    
- **Reading:** *The Truthful Art*, Chapter 11: "Uncertainty and Significance" (How to not lie with data).
    
- **Activity:** Pin-up critique of the A2 Data Posters.
    
- **Assessment:** Legibility at 1 meter vs. 10cm. Is the "Insight" visible within 5 seconds?
    

---

### Phase 2: The Moving Image (Video & Motion)

_Goal: Introduce time as a variable to create narrative pacing._

**Week 5: Time as a Variable**

- **Topic:** Transitioning from "Snapshot" (Poster) to "Movie" (Video). The ethics of motion.
    
- **Reading:** * Designing Interface Animation*, Chapter 1: "Motion in Design Systems."
    
- **Studio Exercise:** _Keyframing Change._ Using After Effects, animate one chart from the poster. How does a bar chart "grow"? Does it pop, slide, or fade?
    
- **Assignment:** Draft a storyboard for a 30-second "Data Short Film." (In-class Session)
- **Assignment:** Choose a data source for your final jury (Refer to Presentation Week 05).
    

**Week 6: Narrative Structure in Data**

- **Topic:** Storytelling arcs (Setup, Conflict/Insight, Resolution) applied to abstract numbers.
    
- **Reading:** * The Truthful Art*, Chapter 3: "The Beauty of Truth" (Focus on narrative flow).
    
- **Studio Exercise:** _The 6-Second Vine._ Create a loop that explains one single statistic perfectly in 6 seconds (GIF format).
    
- **Assignment:** Refine storyboards and begin asset generation.
    

**Week 7: Motion Production & Easing**

- **Topic:** Kinetic Typography. Making text move to emphasize meaning.
    
- **Reading:** * Designing Interface Animation*, Chapter 3: "The 12 Principles of UX in Motion."
    
- **Studio Exercise:** _Easing and Flow._ Workshop on animation curves (Graph Editor)—making data movement feel "organic" rather than "robotic."
    
- **Assignment:** Finalize the visual track of the video (Silent draft).


**Week 8: Midterm Review (The Silent Screening)**

- **Activity:** Watch all 30-second data films without audio.
    
- **Discussion:** Does the visual narrative hold up on its own? Is the typography readable in motion?
    
- **Deliverable:** 30-Second Motion Graphics Video (Visuals locked).
    

---

### Phase 3: The Sonic Layer (Sound Design & Sonification)

_Goal: Use sound to convey information (Sonification) and emotion (Soundscape)._

**Week 9: Introduction to Soundscapes**

- **Topic:** Acoustic Ecology and the sonic environment.
    
- **Reading:** * The Soundscape*, Introduction & Chapter 1: "The Soundscape."
    
- **Studio Exercise:** _The Soundwalk._ Students record the "keynote" sounds of the campus.
    
- **Technical Lab:** Introduction to Audacity/Reaper for basic layering.
    

**Week 10: Data Sonification (Hearing Numbers)**

- **Topic:** Parameter Mapping (Mapping data values to Pitch, Volume, or Tempo).
    
- **Reading:** * The Soundscape*, Chapter 10: "The Acoustic Community" (Focus on signals vs. noise).
    
- **Studio Exercise:** _TwoTone Workshop._ Use the tool **TwoTone.io** to convert the CSV data (from Week 2) into a MIDI melody. High value = High pitch.
    
- **Assignment:** Generate a "Data Melody" that matches the duration of the video.
    

**Week 11: The Audio-Visual Sync**

- **Topic:** Synchronization and "Mickey Mousing" (matching action to beat).
    
- **Studio Exercise:** _The Mix._ Students import their "Data Melody" and "Soundscape" layers into their video projects.
    
- **Critique:** "Blind Listening." Listen to the audio tracks without video. Can we "hear" the trend of the data?
    
- **Assignment:** Final Video with Audio mixdown.
    

---

### Phase 4: The Digital Container (Web & Portfolio)

_Goal: Use Static Site Generators to host the case study (Portfolio)._

**Week 12: Architecture of the Archive**

- **Topic:** Static Sites (Jekyll) and Markdown. The environmental impact of web design.
    
- **Reading:** * Sustainable Web Design*, Chapter 1: "Internet Energy" & Chapter 4: "Page Weight."
    
- **Studio Exercise:** _The Readme._ Write the project case study in Markdown (Title, Problem, Process, Solution) without any styling.
    
- **Assignment:** Set up a GitHub repository and a basic Jekyll theme.
    

**Week 13: Scrollytelling & Interaction**

- **Topic:** Designing for the browser. Embedding media effectively.
    
- **Reading:** * Sustainable Web Design*, Chapter 3: "Design" (Focus on image optimization).
    
- **Studio Exercise:** _The Integration._ Embed the A1 Poster (PDF), the Video (Vimeo/YouTube), and the Audio track into the static site.
    
- **Assignment:** Polish the CSS/Style of the webpage to match the poster's aesthetic.
    

**Week 14: Final Exhibition & Launch**

- **Activity:** "The Multimedia Data Gallery."
    
- **Deliverables:**
    
    1. **Physical:** A1 Poster on the wall.
        
    2. **Digital:** QR Code leading to the Student's Static Web Page (Portfolio Item).
        
    3. **Experience:** The Data Video playing on a loop with headphones for the sound design.
        
- **Final Critique:** Evaluating the consistency of the message across Print, Video, Sound, and Code.

- Start with **small, classic datasets** (Iris, Titanic) to teach chart basics.

- Encourage students to explore **Kaggle competitions** to apply skills in real-world contexts.

# Week 07 - Assignment Mockups
In this stage of your data visualization project, you will move from raw information to structured insight. Before we dive into high-fidelity aesthetics, you must deeply understand the "why" and "how" of your dataset. This week is dedicated to rigorous analysis and structural planning.

### Phase 1: Deep Data Analysis
Analyze your chosen dataset to understand the dynamics and reciprocal relationships between variables. Use various tools to find inspiration and extract meaningful insights.
- **AI as a Research Partner:** Upload your `.csv` data to an LLM (such as Gemini). Use it to infer exclusive insights, identify outliers, or discover hidden correlations.
- **Visual Prototyping:** Ask the AI to generate different charts (scatter plots, heatmaps, etc.) to help you see the data in a clear, visual form.

### Phase 2: Wireframing (3 Variations)
Based on your findings, draft at least **three different wireframe layouts** for your final data visualization.
- **Focus:** Spatial organization and information hierarchy.
- **Constraints:** These layouts should _not_ include final typography, colors, or symbolic objects yet. Focus purely on the composition and where the data "lives" on the screen or page.
    
### Phase 3: Reflection & Submission
Document your process in a blog post on your **Notion site** (Week 07 entry). Include:
1. A summary of the insights you gained during analysis.
2. Images or scans of your three wireframe layouts.
3. A brief explanation of why you chose these specific structural approaches.

**Submit the link to your Notion entry on the LMS.**

# Week 08 - Things getting Serious

## 📑 Assignment Brief: Visualising Global CO₂ Emissions

**Title:** _Telling Stories with Data – CO₂ Emissions Across Nations_

**Objective:**  
Students will create a multi-layered visualisation of global CO₂ emissions using the _Our World in Data_ dataset. The goal is to combine technical proficiency with narrative clarity, showing how visual design choices shape interpretation.

---

### Tasks
1. **Data Preparation**
    - Download the dataset from _Our World in Data – CO₂ Emissions_.
    - Clean and organise the data for at least **five countries** across a **50-year period**.
2. **Visualisation**
    - Produce **three distinct chart types** (e.g., line chart, stacked bar chart, choropleth map).
    - Ensure each chart has clear titles, labels, and legends.
    - Apply consistent colour schemes and typographic hierarchy.
3. **Narrative Layer**
    - Write a **150–200 word caption** for each chart explaining:
        - What the chart shows.
        - Why the chosen visual form is effective.
        - What story or insight emerges from the data.
4. **Critical Reflection**
    - Submit a **short reflection (300 words)** discussing:
        - How design choices influence perception.
        - What limitations exist in the dataset.
        - How visualisation can contribute to public discourse on climate change.

### Deliverables
- Blog week08 containing:
    - Three charts with captions.
    - Reflection text.
- All source files (Illustrator/Excel/Data/etc.) neatly organised on your Google Drive
