# Mapping Domestic Sonic Ecology: Field Recording Protocols and Soundscape Analysis

# Domestic Soundscapes: Sensory Ethnography from Fieldwork to Archive

Protocols from Field Capture to Audio Archive


## Project Workflow Breakdown

- **Define Scope**    
    - Clarify research questions: How do domestic spaces shape sensory experience?
        
    - Identify boundaries: Which households, rooms, or everyday practices will be studied?
        
- **Design Data Schema**
    - Decide what types of data to collect (audio, notes, photos, contextual metadata).
        
    - Create metadata templates (e.g., date, location, equipment, participant role).
        
    - Standardize file naming conventions for consistency.
        
- **Prepare Recording Protocols**
    - Select recording devices and formats (e.g., WAV, 48kHz).
        
    - Establish guidelines for field recording (placement, duration, ambient noise).
        
    - Draft consent and ethical documentation for participants.
        
- **Conduct Sensory Ethnography**
    - Spend time in domestic spaces observing sensory practices.
        
    - Record soundscapes, everyday routines, and interactions.
        
    - Take reflexive notes on your own sensory experience.

	- Keep in your mind the methods of soundscape theories (E.g. Categorization + SCT analysis) 
        
- **Organize Collected Data**
    - Input recordings into your schema with metadata tags.
        
    - Store files in a structured archive (folders by household, date, or theme).
        
    - Ensure backups and version control.

	- Write structured and detailed sentences describing your aural experience.
        
- **Initial Review**
    - Listen back to recordings for quality and relevance. Compare with your definitions from the previous step.
        
    - Annotate key moments (e.g., sonic events, domestic rituals).
        
    - Identify gaps or areas needing further documentation.
